Definitions
What is clienteling?

Clienteling is the practice of sales associates building personal, ongoing relationships with individual customers — remembering their preferences, sizes, purchase history and style — and using that knowledge to make personalized recommendations, rather than treating every customer interaction as anonymous.

Where it comes from

The term has its roots in luxury retail, where the best sales associates have always kept mental (or paper) notes on their regular clients — what they bought last season, their sizing, what they'd likely want next. Clienteling software digitizes that practice, so it scales beyond what one associate can remember and survives staff turnover.

How it works today

Modern clienteling software gives each associate a digital client book — profiles with purchase history, preferences and past interactions — plus tools to act on that data: automated alerts about which clients to reach out to, shoppable content to send them, and a way to track whether the outreach led to a sale.

Clienteling vs. CRM

A CRM (customer relationship management system) is a broader system of record for tracking any kind of customer or sales relationship, across any industry. Clienteling is more specific: it's about individual, personalized relationship-building — usually in retail — often powered by a CRM underneath, but focused on the associate-to-client relationship rather than the sales pipeline as a whole.
